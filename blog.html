How to Effectively Control Hair Fall: Causes, Nutrition & Clinical PRP/GFC Solutions

Losing 50 to 100 strands a day is natural, but when hair clogs the shower drain or thinning visibly widens your parting line, quick cosmetic shampoos won't fix it. Understand the difference between temporary Telogen Effluvium and genetic pattern hair loss, the vital role of ferritin and Vitamin D3, and how autologous PRP/GFC therapies reactivate dormant hair follicles.

Hair fall is one of the most common complaints we treat at Dr. Rui's Clinic. In urban environments like Pune, high stress, hard water, pollution, and nutritional gaps frequently trigger excessive hair shedding.

1. Understanding the Root Causes

Hair loss broadly falls into two clinical categories:

  • Telogen Effluvium: Diffuse sudden shedding typically occurring 2 to 3 months after physical illness, intense emotional stress, rapid weight loss, or childbirth.
  • Androgenetic Alopecia (Pattern Thinning): Progressive follicle miniaturization driven by genetic sensitivity to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), resulting in hairline recession in men or a widened crown parting in women.

2. Nutritional & Laboratory Screenings

Before buying expensive hair serums, we check your internal biomarkers. Over 70% of hair fall patients in India have low Serum Ferritin (iron reserves), Vitamin D3 insufficiency, or subclinical thyroid imbalances. Correcting these deficiencies provides the biological foundation your hair roots need to grow.

3. Clinical Regeneration: PRP & GFC Therapy

When topical treatments alone prove slow, Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) and Growth Factor Concentrate (GFC) provide a breakthrough. By isolating autologous growth factors from your own blood and delivering them directly to follicle stem cells, we stimulate microvascular circulation, prolong the Anagen (growth) phase, and reverse miniaturization without synthetic drugs.

4. Daily Hair Care Rules to Follow

  • Wash your scalp 2-3 times weekly with a gentle, sulfate-free pH-balanced cleanser to prevent sebum buildup and dandruff.
  • Never vigorously towel-dry wet hair or comb damp strands with a fine-toothed comb.
  • Avoid continuous high-heat blow drying directly against your scalp roots.

The Complete Dermatologist-Approved Acne Care Routine for Indian Skin

Indian skin has active sebaceous glands and is particularly prone to Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)—meaning even a small pimple can leave a dark mark for months. Learn why aggressive scrubs and homemade lemon concoctions ruin your skin barrier, and discover the essential 3-step clinical routine that clears active breakouts safely.

Acne is not merely a cosmetic inconvenience; it is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the pilosebaceous unit. Because melanin-rich Indian skin reacts intensely to inflammation, treating acne promptly is critical to prevent deep scarring and hyperpigmentation.

1. Ditch Harmful DIY Remedies

Common household remedies like lemon juice, raw baking soda, or toothpaste disrupt the skin's acidic mantle (pH 4.5–5.5), causing chemical micro-burns and triggering severe post-inflammatory dark marks. Always choose medical-grade, pH-balanced topicals.

2. The Dermatologist-Approved 3-Step Routine

  • Step 1 (Cleanse): Use a gentle salicylic acid (1-2%) or glycolic cleanser morning and evening to dissolve trapped sebum and dead keratin cells inside pores.
  • Step 2 (Treat & Hydrate): Many acne patients mistakenly avoid moisturizers. Dehydrated skin compensates by producing more oil! Apply an oil-free, non-comedogenic gel containing hyaluronic acid or niacinamide (4-5%) to soothe redness and restore the lipid barrier.
  • Step 3 (Sun Protection): UV radiation oxidizes sebum and darkens post-acne blemishes into stubborn black marks. Wear a broad-spectrum, non-greasy matte sunscreen (SPF 50 PA++++) daily.

3. When to See a Dermatologist for Clinical Peels

If cystic pimples or painful nodules persist for over 2 weeks, consult a specialist. At Dr. Rui's Clinic, procedures like our medical Black Acetic Acid Peel and targeted extraction calm inflammation within days, stopping scarring before it begins.

Melasma & Stubborn Pigmentation: Root Causes, Laser Treatments & Daily Sun Protection

Melasma often shows up as brownish-grey symmetrical patches across the cheekbones, bridge of the nose, and forehead. Driven by estrogen fluctuations, sun exposure, and screen blue light, understanding its biology is the key to fading dark patches permanently without rebound hyperpigmentation.

Melasma is one of the most stubborn pigmentary challenges in cosmetology. Unlike simple sun freckles which sit on the outermost epidermal layer, melasma often involves both epidermal and deep dermal melanophages accompanied by fragile microvasculature.

1. Key Triggers You Must Know

  • Hormonal Shifts: Pregnancy (chloasma), oral contraceptives, and thyroid imbalances stimulate melanocyte-stimulating hormone receptors.
  • UV Radiation & Visible Light: Both ultraviolet A/B rays and high-energy visible blue light from digital displays stimulate pigment-producing cells.
  • Heat Exposure: Infrared radiation and cooking steam can trigger inflammatory pathways that aggravate melasma patches.

2. Clinical Treatment: Peels & Q-Switched Lasers

At Dr. Rui's Clinic, we avoid aggressive ablative treatments that can trigger rebound post-inflammatory pigmentation. Instead, we use gentle Q-Switched Nd:YAG laser toning in low-fluence sub-photothermal modes to fragment pigment granules, coupled with customized tranexamic and kojic acid chemical peels to prevent future melanin synthesis.

3. The Non-Negotiable Sunscreen Protocol

Standard sunscreens protect only against UV rays, but melasma requires protection against visible light too. Look for broad-spectrum sunscreens containing iron oxides, zinc oxide, and titanium dioxide. Reapply every 3 to 4 hours when exposed to sunlight.

Doctor's Golden Skin Rules

✓ Never sleep with makeup: Trapped pigments clog pores and accelerate collagen breakdown overnight.
✓ SPF every single day: Even on cloudy Pune afternoons or indoors near windows.
✓ Patch test new serums: Test active acids on your jawline for 24 hours before full-face application.
✓ Hydrate from within: Drink 2.5–3 liters of water daily to maintain skin turgor and cellular detox.
